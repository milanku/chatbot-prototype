# Account Closure, Inactivity, Estates & Power of Attorney

This document explains how Aurora Bank handles account closure, inactive accounts, deceased customers, legal representatives, and powers of attorney.

## 1. Customer-requested account closure

A customer may ask Aurora Bank to close an account through online banking, branch, telephone banking, or written instruction. Aurora Bank may require identity verification before closure.

Before closure, the customer should:

- download required statements;
- cancel or move direct debits and standing orders;
- update salary, pension, benefits, and merchant payment details;
- repay overdrafts, fees, interest, loans, or card balances linked to the account;
- withdraw or transfer remaining funds;
- destroy cards and cheques where applicable;
- provide a destination account for closing balance.

Savings accounts, notice accounts, fixed-term deposits, loans, credit cards, and packaged benefits may have separate closure terms.

## 2. Closure restrictions

Aurora Bank may refuse or delay closure where:

- the account has a negative balance;
- there are pending card transactions, payment disputes, or pre-authorizations;
- a court order, tax order, enforcement order, or sanctions restriction applies;
- fraud, money laundering, scam, or complaint investigation is ongoing;
- a fixed-term product has not matured;
- a joint account holder has not consented where required;
- legal authority for a representative is incomplete;
- funds are subject to estate, insolvency, or guardianship processes.

We will explain the reason where legally allowed.

## 3. Bank-initiated closure

Aurora Bank may close an account by giving notice under the account terms. We may close immediately or restrict services where required because of:

- fraud or suspected fraud;
- threatening or abusive conduct;
- illegal or prohibited account use;
- sanctions or legal requirements;
- false information;
- account used for business activity contrary to personal-account terms;
- failure to provide required information;
- serious breach of terms;
- risk to bank systems or other customers.

Where possible, remaining funds will be returned to the customer after legal and security checks.

## 4. Switching away from Aurora Bank

If the customer switches to another bank, Aurora Bank will cooperate with an approved switching scheme where available. The customer should ensure that payments, salary, direct debits, and standing orders are transferred correctly.

Payments received after closure may be returned to the sender unless a switching redirection service applies.

## 5. Inactive accounts

An account may be marked inactive after 12 months without customer-initiated activity or meaningful customer contact. Activity can include a customer payment, card transaction, branch visit, secure message, login with confirmed identity, or signed instruction.

Automated interest, fees, or bank-initiated entries do not always count as customer activity.

Aurora Bank may reduce statements, block new cards, lower limits, or request updated customer information on inactive accounts.

## 6. Dormant accounts

An account may become dormant after a longer inactivity period defined by law or product terms. Aurora Bank will attempt to contact the customer before applying dormant-account processes. Dormant accounts may require enhanced verification before reactivation.

Dormant funds remain the customer’s property unless local unclaimed-property law requires transfer to a government or designated dormant asset scheme.

## 7. Reactivating an inactive or dormant account

To reactivate an account, a customer may need to:

- verify identity and address;
- update tax residency and contact information;
- confirm source of funds and expected account use;
- reset online banking credentials;
- request a replacement card;
- visit a branch if remote verification is not sufficient.

If the account is obsolete, Aurora Bank may require migration to a current product.

## 8. Deceased customers

When Aurora Bank is notified that a customer has died, we may freeze sole accounts to protect estate funds. For joint accounts, operation may continue for surviving account holders unless legal restrictions apply, but the deceased customer’s name will be removed after required checks.

We may request:

- death certificate or official death record;
- identity documents for the person notifying us;
- grant of probate, letters of administration, certificate of inheritance, or equivalent document;
- will or court document where relevant;
- tax or estate information required by law;
- payment instructions for funeral expenses or estate distribution.

Aurora Bank may release limited funds for funeral expenses or inheritance-tax payments where law and policy permit.

## 9. Estates and account closure

Estate processing depends on account value, product type, jurisdiction, and legal documentation. Small estates may qualify for a simplified process. Larger or disputed estates require formal documents.

Loans, overdrafts, credit cards, and negative balances remain debts of the estate unless insurance or law provides otherwise. Savings, current account balances, and deposits may be paid to the estate representative after verification.

## 10. Power of attorney

A power of attorney allows an appointed person to act for the customer. Aurora Bank must review the document before allowing access. We may require certified copies, identity documents for the attorney, specimen signatures, and confirmation that the document is valid and in force.

An attorney may be allowed to:

- view statements;
- make payments;
- manage cards where permitted;
- update contact details for account servicing;
- close accounts where permitted;
- speak with customer service.

An attorney may not use the customer’s funds for the attorney’s own benefit unless legally authorized. Aurora Bank may refuse instructions that appear abusive, conflicted, outside authority, or inconsistent with the customer’s interests.

## 11. Guardianship, curatorship, and court orders

Where a court appoints a guardian, curator, deputy, or similar representative, Aurora Bank will follow the court order. The representative’s powers may be limited to certain accounts, amounts, or transaction types.

Aurora Bank may require updated orders or evidence that authority continues.

## 12. Joint account disputes and separation

If joint account holders disagree, Aurora Bank may restrict the account and require joint instructions. This can affect withdrawals, card use, overdrafts, statements, and closure.

In cases of domestic or financial abuse, Aurora Bank will consider safe contact methods and support options. See [Complaints, Vulnerable Customers & Accessibility](10_complaints_vulnerable_customers_and_accessibility.md).

## 13. Data after account closure

Aurora Bank retains account and transaction records after closure for legal, regulatory, tax, accounting, fraud prevention, and dispute reasons. Data retention is described in [Privacy, Data Protection & Open Banking](08_privacy_data_protection_and_open_banking.md).
