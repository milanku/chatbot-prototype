# Account Opening, Eligibility & Customer Verification Policy

This document explains how Aurora Bank opens customer accounts, verifies customers, and reviews applications. It is written for customers applying for personal banking products. Lending-specific requirements are described in [Loans, Overdrafts & Credit Assessment](05_loans_overdrafts_and_credit_assessment.md).

## 1. Who can apply

Aurora Bank normally accepts applications from natural persons who meet all of the following requirements:

- are at least 18 years old for a standard personal current account or savings account;
- have legal capacity to enter into a banking relationship;
- can provide valid proof of identity and proof of residential address;
- can explain the expected purpose and use of the account;
- are not subject to legal or sanctions restrictions that prevent us from providing services;
- agree to the account terms, privacy notice, electronic communications terms, and applicable fee schedule.

Applicants aged 15 to 17 may apply for a Youth Current Account with consent from a parent or legal guardian. Youth accounts have reduced limits, no arranged overdraft, and no credit-card eligibility.

Aurora Bank may offer a Basic Payment Account to customers who meet legal eligibility criteria but may not qualify for a standard packaged account. Basic accounts provide essential payment services but do not include credit facilities.

## 2. Residency and address requirements

A customer does not always need to be a citizen of the country where the account is opened. However, Aurora Bank must be able to verify the customer’s identity, address, tax residency, and expected account activity.

We may request additional information where:

- the customer recently moved to the country;
- the customer lives in one country but receives income in another;
- the customer has more than one tax residency;
- the customer is a student, posted worker, diplomat, cross-border employee, or seasonal worker;
- the customer cannot provide a standard utility bill or lease agreement;
- the customer’s address is temporary, shared, institutional, or a correspondence-only address.

A post-office box or virtual mailbox is not accepted as the sole residential address. A correspondence address can be added after a residential address has been verified.

## 3. Documents we may request

Aurora Bank must verify the identity of every account holder and certain related persons. Documents may be uploaded in online banking, presented in branch, or verified through an approved digital identity process.

### 3.1 Proof of identity

We normally accept one of the following:

| Document type | Requirements |
|---|---|
| Passport | Must be valid, readable, signed where required, and show photograph and date of birth. |
| National identity card | Must be valid and issued by a recognized authority. |
| Residence permit | May be accepted together with a passport or national identity card. |
| Digital identity certificate | Accepted only when issued through an Aurora-approved identity scheme. |

Expired identity documents are normally not accepted. We may accept an expired document only where local law requires a transition period and the document can still be independently validated.

### 3.2 Proof of address

We normally accept one of the following dated within the last 90 days:

- utility bill for electricity, gas, water, fixed broadband, or municipal services;
- bank statement issued by a regulated financial institution;
- lease agreement with landlord details and property address;
- official tax, social security, or municipal letter;
- employment confirmation showing a residential address, only where accompanied by another supporting document;
- university housing confirmation for students.

Mobile phone bills, handwritten letters, parcel receipts, and screenshots that do not show the issuer and date are not normally sufficient.

### 3.3 Source of funds and source of wealth

For some customers, Aurora Bank may ask how money was earned or accumulated. This is not a loan assessment; it is a financial-crime control. Acceptable evidence can include payslips, employment contracts, tax returns, sale agreements, inheritance documents, dividend statements, grant letters, or business invoices.

We may ask for source-of-funds information if an account is expected to receive unusually large transfers, cash deposits, third-party payments, crypto-asset proceeds, foreign-source income, or payments inconsistent with the stated account purpose.

## 4. Application channels

Customers may apply through:

1. Aurora Online Banking or the mobile application;
2. an Aurora branch or partner branch;
3. telephone-assisted onboarding for customers who need accessibility support;
4. a workplace, student, or relocation onboarding program approved by Aurora Bank.

Online applications can usually be completed without visiting a branch. We may still require branch verification if documents are unclear, fraud indicators are present, or the customer is applying for a product with enhanced checks.

## 5. What happens during verification

Aurora Bank may carry out the following checks:

- identity document validation;
- address verification;
- sanctions and politically exposed person screening;
- fraud database and internal risk checks;
- credit reference agency checks where a credit facility is requested;
- tax residency and common reporting standard checks;
- review of adverse media or publicly available information where relevant to financial-crime obligations.

For non-credit accounts, credit bureau information is not normally used to decide whether the customer can open a basic current account, but it may be used to verify identity or assess an overdraft request.

## 6. Account purpose and expected activity

We will ask why the customer wants the account and what types of transactions are expected. Examples include salary receipt, rent and bills, family support, savings, travel spending, student living expenses, pension income, or general day-to-day banking.

Customers must not use a personal account for business trading, client money, payment processing on behalf of others, unregistered charity activity, or money-service activity. Sole traders and freelancers may need a separate business account if business income and expenses are regular or material.

## 7. Joint accounts

A joint account is owned by two or more customers. Each account holder must complete identity and address verification. Unless the account mandate says otherwise, any one account holder may give payment instructions, use a debit card issued in their own name, view statements, and close certain services.

Important rules for joint accounts:

- all account holders are jointly and severally responsible for fees, overdrafts, and negative balances;
- one account holder’s debt or legal restriction may affect the account;
- account holders should agree privately how money in the account is owned, because Aurora Bank does not normally decide beneficial shares;
- if there is a dispute between account holders, Aurora Bank may restrict withdrawals or require joint instructions;
- a joint account is not suitable where customers cannot trust each other to operate the account responsibly.

A joint account may be converted to a sole account only with the consent of all account holders and subject to product eligibility. If one account holder dies, the process in [Account Closure, Inactivity, Estates & Power of Attorney](11_account_closure_inactivity_estates_and_power_of_attorney.md) applies.

## 8. Youth accounts and accounts for minors

A Youth Current Account is available to customers aged 15 to 17 with guardian consent. The guardian must verify their identity and relationship to the minor. Youth accounts do not offer overdrafts, credit cards, cheque facilities, or high-value international payments.

When the customer turns 18, Aurora Bank may convert the account to an adult account after providing notice. The customer may need to provide updated identity, tax residency, and address information.

## 9. Customers who need support

Aurora Bank will make reasonable adjustments for customers who cannot complete standard onboarding because of disability, illness, digital exclusion, language barriers, domestic abuse, homelessness, displacement, or other vulnerability. Examples include branch appointments, interpreter support, alternative documents, large-print forms, and trusted-contact procedures.

Support options are described in [Complaints, Vulnerable Customers & Accessibility](10_complaints_vulnerable_customers_and_accessibility.md).

## 10. Reasons an application may be delayed

An application may take longer where:

- documents are unclear, expired, incomplete, inconsistent, or not in an accepted language;
- identity information does not match public or credit reference records;
- the customer has a complex residency or tax position;
- additional source-of-funds information is required;
- a joint applicant has not completed their checks;
- sanctions, fraud, or legal-order screening requires manual review;
- the requested product includes credit, insurance, investments, or other regulated features.

We will normally tell the customer what information is missing, unless law or security rules prevent us from doing so.

## 11. Reasons we may refuse an application

Aurora Bank may refuse to open an account where:

- identity cannot be verified;
- required documents are not provided;
- the customer provides false, misleading, or incomplete information;
- the account appears likely to be used for unlawful activity;
- sanctions, court orders, or regulatory restrictions apply;
- the customer has previously misused Aurora Bank services;
- the product is not suitable or available in the customer’s location;
- the customer does not agree to the required terms.

Where possible, we will give a general reason for refusal. We may not disclose detailed fraud, sanctions, or risk-screening reasons.

## 12. Updating information after opening

Customers must keep their information up to date. They should tell Aurora Bank promptly when their name, address, phone number, email address, tax residency, citizenship, employment, expected account use, or beneficial ownership status changes.

Aurora Bank may restrict certain services if a customer does not respond to reasonable information requests. We will not ask customers to disclose passwords, one-time passcodes, full card PINs, or remote-access credentials.

## 13. Account activation

An account is normally activated when:

1. the account holder’s identity is verified;
2. all required declarations are accepted;
3. the customer receives account details;
4. any initial deposit requirement is met;
5. the customer sets up secure access credentials.

Debit cards and online banking access may be issued separately. Credit cards, overdrafts, and loans require separate approval.
