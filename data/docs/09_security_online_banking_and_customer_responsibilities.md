# Security, Online Banking & Customer Responsibilities

This document explains how Aurora Bank protects digital banking and what customers must do to keep accounts safe. Fraud claim handling is described in [Disputes, Chargebacks & Fraud Claims](07_disputes_chargebacks_and_fraud_claims.md).

## 1. Security principles

Aurora Bank uses layered security controls to protect accounts. These controls may include encryption, device binding, login monitoring, fraud scoring, strong customer authentication, payment warnings, transaction limits, account locks, and manual review.

Security controls may occasionally delay payments, block card use, restrict online access, or require additional verification. Aurora Bank will not compensate for inconvenience where a control was applied reasonably for safety or legal compliance.

## 2. Strong customer authentication

Strong customer authentication uses at least two independent elements from the following categories:

- something the customer knows, such as password or PIN;
- something the customer has, such as registered device or hardware token;
- something the customer is, such as fingerprint or facial recognition.

Some low-risk transactions may be exempt, but Aurora Bank may still require authentication based on fraud risk, transaction amount, merchant type, new device, new payee, unusual behavior, or regulatory rules.

## 3. What Aurora Bank will never ask for

Aurora Bank will never ask a customer to:

- disclose a full card PIN;
- disclose a full online banking password;
- reveal a one-time passcode to approve a transaction the customer did not create;
- move money to a safe account;
- ignore security warnings;
- install remote-access software because of an unsolicited call;
- give a card to a courier;
- lie to branch staff or other banks about the purpose of a payment;
- send money to receive a refund, prize, job, loan, or investment withdrawal.

A request like this should be treated as suspicious.

## 4. Customer security duties

Customers must take reasonable steps to keep accounts safe, including:

- keeping cards, devices, PINs, passwords, passcodes, and security tokens secure;
- using unique passwords for banking;
- keeping phone numbers and email addresses up to date;
- reviewing statements and notifications;
- reporting suspicious activity promptly;
- using trusted networks and updated devices;
- logging out of shared devices;
- not sharing credentials with family, friends, merchants, or third-party apps unless the process is an approved open banking flow;
- not bypassing bank warnings or controls.

Failure to follow these duties can affect liability for unauthorized transactions.

## 5. Phishing, smishing, and vishing

Phishing uses fake emails, websites, or messages. Smishing uses SMS or chat messages. Vishing uses phone calls. Scammers may pretend to be Aurora Bank, police, tax authorities, delivery companies, investment platforms, employers, or family members.

Customers should check:

- whether the message creates urgency or fear;
- whether the link domain is genuine;
- whether the caller asks for credentials or payment approval;
- whether the request matches normal bank behavior;
- whether contact details came from a known official source.

If uncertain, customers should stop the interaction and contact Aurora Bank using a known channel from [Glossary, Contact Channels & Service Levels](12_glossary_contact_and_service_level.md).

## 6. Payment warnings

Aurora Bank may show warnings before high-risk payments. Customers should read warnings carefully. If a warning says the payment may be a scam, the customer should pause and verify independently.

A customer should not continue a payment because someone on the phone instructs them to do so. Scammers often coach victims to bypass warnings or mislead the bank.

## 7. New payee and payment verification

When setting up a new payee, customers should verify the account number through a trusted channel. For invoices, the customer should confirm changes to bank details using a known phone number, not contact details on the changed invoice.

Aurora Bank may delay, hold, or reject first payments to new payees, high-risk merchants, crypto-asset platforms, or countries with elevated risk.

## 8. Device security

Customers should keep devices secure by:

- using screen lock and biometric protection;
- installing operating system and app updates;
- using anti-malware tools where appropriate;
- avoiding jailbroken or rooted devices for banking;
- downloading apps only from official stores;
- revoking access for lost or old devices;
- not approving login prompts they did not initiate.

Aurora Bank may block access from devices that appear compromised.

## 9. Account locks and access recovery

Aurora Bank may lock online banking after repeated failed login attempts, suspected account takeover, device compromise, fraud reports, legal restrictions, or security maintenance.

To recover access, the customer may need to complete identity checks, reset credentials, confirm recent activity, update contact details, or visit a branch. We may refuse remote recovery if identity cannot be verified safely.

## 10. Secure messages and document upload

Customers should send sensitive information only through approved secure channels. Documents uploaded to online banking should be clear, complete, and unaltered. Aurora Bank may reject documents that are cropped, blurry, password-protected, edited, or inconsistent.

Customers should not send identity documents, card numbers, passwords, or one-time passcodes through ordinary email unless specifically instructed through a secure process.

## 11. Social media and public channels

Aurora Bank may provide general support through social media but will not conduct full account servicing through public comments. Customers should not post account numbers, card numbers, personal documents, transaction details, or complaint evidence publicly.

## 12. If a customer suspects fraud

The customer should:

1. stop communicating with the suspected scammer;
2. freeze affected cards if possible;
3. change online banking password from a secure device;
4. contact Aurora Bank using a known channel;
5. report unauthorized transactions;
6. preserve evidence such as messages, websites, phone numbers, wallet addresses, invoices, and screenshots;
7. report to police or national cybercrime body where appropriate.

Fraud claims and disputes are handled under [Disputes, Chargebacks & Fraud Claims](07_disputes_chargebacks_and_fraud_claims.md).

## 13. Third-party access and open banking

Customers should not share banking credentials with budgeting apps or third-party providers outside the official open banking process. Open banking permissions are described in [Privacy, Data Protection & Open Banking](08_privacy_data_protection_and_open_banking.md).

## 14. Security-related service interruptions

Aurora Bank may suspend or limit digital services during security incidents, cyberattacks, emergency maintenance, payment-scheme incidents, or third-party outages. We will provide updates where possible without compromising security.
