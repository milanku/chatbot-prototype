# Payments, Transfers & Cutoff Times

This document explains how Aurora Bank processes payments and transfers. It should be read with [Fees, Interest & Foreign Exchange](06_fees_interest_and_exchange_rates.md), [Security, Online Banking & Customer Responsibilities](09_security_online_banking_and_customer_responsibilities.md), and [Disputes, Chargebacks & Fraud Claims](07_disputes_chargebacks_and_fraud_claims.md).

## 1. Payment types supported

Aurora Bank personal accounts may support the following payment types, depending on product eligibility and channel access.

| Payment type | Typical use | Typical currency | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| SEPA Credit Transfer | Standard euro transfer within SEPA | EUR | Normally arrives by the next business day after execution. |
| SEPA Instant Credit Transfer | Fast euro transfer within participating banks | EUR | Usually processed within seconds, subject to limits and screening. |
| Internal transfer | Transfer between Aurora Bank accounts | EUR or account currency | Usually immediate unless held for checks. |
| Standing order | Repeating customer-initiated payment | EUR | Customer controls amount, recipient, and schedule. |
| Direct debit | Merchant or organization collects payment after customer authorization | EUR | Customer can cancel mandate; merchant contract may remain. |
| International wire | Cross-border transfer outside SEPA or in non-euro currency | Multiple | Additional information, fees, FX spread, and correspondent banks may apply. |
| Card payment | Purchase or withdrawal using debit or credit card | Multiple | Authorization and final settlement may differ. |

Not all payment types are available on youth, savings, basic, or restricted accounts.

## 2. Business days and bank holidays

A business day is a day on which Aurora Bank and the relevant payment system are open for processing. Saturdays, Sundays, public holidays, TARGET closing days, and local bank holidays may not be business days for some payment types.

A payment instructed outside business hours, after a cutoff time, on a weekend, or on a bank holiday is normally treated as received on the next business day.

## 3. Cutoff times

Cutoff times are shown in Central European Time unless otherwise stated. They can change during public holidays, system maintenance, or payment-scheme disruption.

| Payment type | Standard cutoff | Execution expectation |
|---|---:|---|
| SEPA Credit Transfer submitted in online banking | 15:30 on a business day | Sent same business day; usually credited by beneficiary bank by next business day. |
| SEPA Credit Transfer submitted in branch | 14:00 on a business day | Sent same business day if accepted before cutoff. |
| SEPA Instant Credit Transfer | 24/7 where available | Usually within seconds; may fall back or be declined if unavailable. |
| Internal transfer between own Aurora accounts | 24/7 | Usually immediate. |
| Internal transfer to another Aurora customer | 22:00 daily | Usually same day after security checks. |
| International wire in EUR | 13:00 on a business day | Usually 1-2 business days, depending on route. |
| International wire in non-EUR currency | 11:30 on a business day | Usually 2-5 business days, depending on currency and intermediaries. |
| Standing order creation or amendment | 18:00 the business day before due date | Changes after cutoff may apply to the next cycle only. |
| Direct debit cancellation | 18:00 the business day before collection date | Later cancellation may not stop the payment. |

Cutoff times are not promises that the beneficiary will receive funds at a specific hour. Intermediary banks, receiving banks, screening, incorrect details, and payment-system availability may affect timing.

## 4. Payment limits

Aurora Bank applies limits to protect customers and comply with law. Limits may vary by product, customer profile, channel, transaction type, and authentication method.

Standard indicative limits for adult personal accounts:

| Channel or payment type | Daily limit | Per-transaction limit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Online SEPA transfer | EUR 25,000 | EUR 15,000 |
| Mobile app SEPA transfer | EUR 10,000 | EUR 5,000 |
| SEPA instant transfer | EUR 5,000 | EUR 2,000 |
| Internal transfer between own accounts | EUR 100,000 | EUR 100,000 |
| ATM cash withdrawal | EUR 600 | EUR 600 |
| Debit card purchase | EUR 5,000 | EUR 3,000 |
| Youth account card purchase | EUR 400 | EUR 200 |

Customers may request a temporary or permanent limit change. Aurora Bank may refuse or reduce limits for security, affordability, account history, product restrictions, or regulatory reasons.

## 5. Required payment information

To make a payment, a customer must provide complete and accurate information. For SEPA transfers, this normally includes:

- beneficiary name;
- IBAN;
- amount;
- payment reference or description;
- execution date;
- optional beneficiary address for some payments.

For international wires, Aurora Bank may also require the beneficiary bank name, BIC/SWIFT code, intermediary bank information, beneficiary address, country, purpose of payment, and supporting documents.

Aurora Bank processes many payments using the account identifier provided. The beneficiary name may not always be checked by the receiving bank. Customers should verify account details before approving a payment.

## 6. Payment authentication

Payments may require strong customer authentication. Authentication can include a password, app confirmation, biometric confirmation, one-time passcode, hardware token, or call-back verification. Aurora Bank will never ask a customer to disclose a full password, full card PIN, or remote-access code.

Additional security checks may delay a payment. Aurora Bank may hold or reject a payment if fraud, sanctions, money laundering, scam, account takeover, or unusual activity indicators are present.

## 7. Future-dated payments and standing orders

A future-dated payment is executed on a date chosen by the customer. A standing order is a repeated future-dated payment. The customer must ensure there is enough available balance before the payment is due.

If there is not enough available balance at the processing time, Aurora Bank may:

- reject the payment;
- retry the payment later the same day;
- create or increase an arranged overdraft if available and permitted;
- create an unarranged overdraft only where permitted by product terms.

Rejected-payment fees may apply. See [Fees, Interest & Foreign Exchange](06_fees_interest_and_exchange_rates.md).

## 8. Direct debits

A direct debit allows an organization to collect money from the customer’s account under a mandate. The customer should receive advance notice from the payee unless the scheme rules allow otherwise.

Aurora Bank can cancel a direct debit mandate on the account, but this does not cancel the underlying contract with the merchant. The merchant may still claim the amount by another method or treat the customer as overdue.

Where a direct debit is unauthorized or collected incorrectly, the customer may have refund rights under applicable payment-scheme and legal rules. Dispute processes are explained in [Disputes, Chargebacks & Fraud Claims](07_disputes_chargebacks_and_fraud_claims.md).

## 9. Card payment authorization and settlement

A card transaction usually has two stages:

1. **Authorization**: the merchant requests approval and the available balance is reduced.
2. **Settlement**: the merchant presents the final transaction and it posts to the account.

The settlement amount can differ from the authorization amount because of tips, currency conversion, fuel pre-authorizations, hotel deposits, delayed transit fares, or partial shipments. Card rules are described in [Cards, Debit Cards, Credit Cards, ATMs & Mobile Wallets](04_cards_debit_credit_atm_mobile_wallets.md).

## 10. International payments and foreign exchange

International payments may involve intermediary banks that deduct fees before the payment reaches the beneficiary. Exchange rates can change between quote, authorization, and settlement. Aurora Bank’s FX policy is described in [Fees, Interest & Foreign Exchange](06_fees_interest_and_exchange_rates.md).

Customers should include a clear purpose of payment for international transfers. Payments may be delayed or returned if the purpose is missing, unclear, or inconsistent with legal requirements.

## 11. Cancelling, recalling, or amending a payment

Cancellation rights depend on payment type.

| Payment type | Can the customer cancel? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Future-dated SEPA payment | Usually yes before cutoff on the business day before execution. | Online cancellation may be available. |
| Standing order | Usually yes before the cutoff for the next due date. | Cancelling does not cancel contracts with merchants. |
| Direct debit | Usually yes before cutoff on the business day before collection. | Customer should also notify the payee. |
| SEPA instant transfer | Usually no once authorized. | Recall can be attempted but is not guaranteed. |
| Standard SEPA transfer | Recall can be requested after execution. | Recipient bank and recipient consent may be required. |
| International wire | Amendment or recall can be requested. | Fees apply and recovery is not guaranteed. |
| Card purchase | Customer should first contact merchant unless fraud is suspected. | Chargeback may be possible under card scheme rules. |

Aurora Bank cannot guarantee recovery of funds sent to the wrong recipient if the payment was authorized by the customer. The bank may try to contact the receiving bank, but the recipient may need to consent to return funds unless legal rules allow reversal.

## 12. Failed, returned, or rejected payments

A payment may fail, return, or be rejected because of:

- insufficient available balance;
- incorrect account details;
- closed or restricted beneficiary account;
- sanctions or fraud screening;
- payment limit exceeded;
- unsupported currency or country;
- missing purpose or beneficiary details;
- receiving-bank refusal;
- scheme outage or technical error.

If a payment is returned, charges may be deducted by other banks. Aurora Bank will credit the returned amount once received and reconciled.

## 13. Payment disputes and unauthorized payments

Customers should report unauthorized or incorrect payments as soon as they notice them. Fast reporting improves the chance of stopping further loss and recovering funds.

Unauthorized electronic payments, scam payments, direct debit errors, card disputes, and merchant chargebacks are handled under [Disputes, Chargebacks & Fraud Claims](07_disputes_chargebacks_and_fraud_claims.md). Security steps are described in [Security, Online Banking & Customer Responsibilities](09_security_online_banking_and_customer_responsibilities.md).

## 14. Payment descriptions and transaction labels

Aurora Bank may show merchant names, payment references, card scheme data, and internal labels in transaction history. A transaction description may differ from the trading name shown on a shopfront or website. Customers can add personal notes or categories in online banking, but personal categories do not change legal payment records.
