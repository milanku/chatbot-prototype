# Disputes, Chargebacks & Fraud Claims

This document explains how Aurora Bank handles transaction disputes, chargebacks, fraud claims, payment recalls, and merchant disputes. Security guidance is in [Security, Online Banking & Customer Responsibilities](09_security_online_banking_and_customer_responsibilities.md).

## 1. Report quickly

Customers should report unauthorized, incorrect, suspicious, or disputed transactions as soon as they notice them. Prompt reporting helps Aurora Bank block further transactions, preserve evidence, and start recovery steps.

A customer should immediately contact Aurora Bank if:

- a card or device is lost or stolen;
- card details, PIN, password, passcode, or authentication device may be compromised;
- a transaction appears that the customer did not authorize;
- the customer was tricked into sending money;
- an ATM debited the account but did not dispense cash;
- a merchant charged the wrong amount or charged after cancellation;
- goods or services were not received;
- a refund promised by a merchant has not arrived.

Emergency contact options are listed in [Glossary, Contact Channels & Service Levels](12_glossary_contact_and_service_level.md).

## 2. Types of disputes

| Dispute type | Examples | Typical first step |
|---|---|---|
| Unauthorized card transaction | Card stolen, card details used online, wallet token compromised | Freeze card and report fraud. |
| Merchant card dispute | Goods not received, defective goods, duplicate charge, cancelled subscription | Contact merchant unless fraud is suspected. |
| ATM dispute | Cash not dispensed, partial dispense, retained card | Report ATM location, date, amount. |
| Direct debit dispute | Wrong amount, no mandate, collected after cancellation | Request direct debit investigation or refund. |
| Bank transfer recall | Payment sent to wrong IBAN or scam recipient | Request recall immediately; recovery not guaranteed. |
| Incoming payment missing | Expected payment not credited | Ask sender for payment proof and trace reference. |
| Fee dispute | Bank fee appears incorrect | Contact customer service with transaction details. |

Different schemes and laws have different deadlines, evidence standards, and refund rules.

## 3. Unauthorized electronic payments

An unauthorized payment is a payment the customer did not consent to. Aurora Bank will investigate whether the payment was authorized, whether strong customer authentication was used, whether the customer acted fraudulently or with gross negligence, and whether the customer reported promptly.

Customers may be liable for losses where they:

- acted fraudulently;
- intentionally or with gross negligence failed to keep security credentials safe;
- delayed reporting after becoming aware of loss, theft, misuse, or unauthorized use;
- gave a card, PIN, passcode, one-time password, or authentication approval to another person;
- ignored clear fraud warnings and authorized a payment anyway.

Aurora Bank will not ask for a full PIN, full password, or remote access. A customer who disclosed credentials because of a scam should still report immediately.

## 4. Card chargebacks

A chargeback is a card-scheme process where Aurora Bank asks the merchant’s bank to return funds because of a valid dispute reason. Chargeback rights depend on card-scheme rules and are not the same as a legal guarantee.

Common chargeback reasons include:

- goods or services not received;
- goods defective or not as described;
- duplicate processing;
- incorrect amount;
- refund not processed;
- cancelled recurring transaction;
- paid by other means;
- ATM cash not dispensed;
- unauthorized transaction.

A chargeback can be challenged by the merchant. Aurora Bank may need evidence from the customer and may reverse a temporary credit if the chargeback is unsuccessful.

## 5. Evidence for merchant disputes

Customers should provide evidence such as:

- order confirmation;
- invoice or receipt;
- merchant terms and cancellation policy;
- delivery tracking;
- photos of defective goods;
- proof of return;
- cancellation confirmation;
- merchant correspondence;
- refund promise;
- police report where relevant;
- travel disruption confirmation for travel claims.

Screenshots should show dates, merchant identity, order number, and full conversation context where possible.

## 6. Time limits

Customers should raise disputes promptly. Card-scheme time limits can be short and may run from transaction date, expected delivery date, cancellation date, or merchant promise date.

Indicative timeframes:

| Claim type | Customer should report by | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Unauthorized card transaction | Immediately, and no later than 13 months where legal rules permit | Faster reporting can reduce loss. |
| Goods or services not received | After expected delivery date, usually within 120 days of expected receipt | Scheme rules vary. |
| Refund not processed | After merchant’s refund timeframe, usually within 120 days of refund promise | Evidence needed. |
| ATM cash not dispensed | Within 30 days if possible | ATM balancing may be time-sensitive. |
| Incorrect direct debit | As soon as noticed | Scheme and legal refund rights may apply. |
| Payment sent to wrong account | Immediately | Recovery becomes harder once funds move. |

The exact deadline depends on scheme and legal rules. Aurora Bank may be unable to pursue a claim filed too late.

## 7. Temporary credits

Aurora Bank may apply a temporary credit while investigating. A temporary credit is not a final decision. It may be reversed if:

- the merchant provides valid evidence;
- the transaction is found to be authorized;
- the customer does not provide requested evidence;
- scheme rules do not support the claim;
- the customer withdraws the dispute;
- the same amount is refunded separately by the merchant.

Customers should not spend a temporary credit unless they understand it may be reversed.

## 8. Scams and authorized push payment fraud

A scam payment occurs when a customer is tricked into authorizing a payment. Examples include impersonation scams, investment scams, romance scams, invoice redirection, purchase scams, and safe-account scams.

Aurora Bank will review scam reports and may attempt recovery from the receiving bank. Refund outcomes depend on law, scheme rules, customer conduct, warning effectiveness, and whether funds remain recoverable. Customers should report immediately and preserve evidence such as messages, phone numbers, websites, invoices, and payment instructions.

If a customer is currently speaking to a suspected scammer, they should end the call or chat and contact Aurora Bank using a known channel, not a number provided by the caller.

## 9. Payment sent to the wrong account

If a customer authorized a transfer to the wrong IBAN or beneficiary, Aurora Bank cannot simply reverse it. We may attempt a recall through the receiving bank. The recipient may need to consent unless legal rules allow otherwise.

The customer should provide:

- payment date and amount;
- beneficiary IBAN and name entered;
- intended beneficiary details;
- reason for error;
- any communication with the unintended recipient.

Recall fees may apply even if recovery is unsuccessful. See [Fees, Interest & Foreign Exchange](06_fees_interest_and_exchange_rates.md).

## 10. Direct debit refunds

If a direct debit was collected without a valid mandate, for the wrong amount, on the wrong date, or after cancellation, the customer should contact Aurora Bank. Aurora Bank may request mandate details and merchant communications.

Cancelling a direct debit with Aurora Bank stops future collections from that mandate where possible. It does not cancel the customer’s contract with the merchant.

## 11. ATM disputes

For an ATM dispute, the customer should provide:

- ATM location and operator;
- date and approximate time;
- amount requested;
- amount dispensed, if any;
- receipt or error message;
- whether the card was retained;
- whether anyone nearby behaved suspiciously.

Aurora Bank may contact the ATM operator and review settlement records. Resolution can take longer for non-Aurora ATMs or ATMs abroad.

## 12. Fraud investigation steps

Aurora Bank may:

- block or replace cards;
- reset online banking credentials;
- suspend payment channels;
- request written statements from the customer;
- request police report references;
- review device, login, and authentication data;
- contact merchants, receiving banks, card schemes, or law enforcement;
- ask the customer to update contact and security settings.

We may not disclose details that could compromise fraud prevention or legal investigations.

## 13. Customer cooperation

Customers must provide truthful and complete information. A claim may be declined if the customer knowingly provides false information, withholds relevant facts, refuses reasonable evidence requests, or makes a duplicate claim after receiving a merchant refund.

Knowingly false fraud claims may lead to account closure, police report, or fraud database filing.

## 14. Complaints about dispute outcomes

If a customer disagrees with a dispute outcome, they can ask for a review and provide additional evidence. If still dissatisfied, they can make a complaint under [Complaints, Vulnerable Customers & Accessibility](10_complaints_vulnerable_customers_and_accessibility.md).
