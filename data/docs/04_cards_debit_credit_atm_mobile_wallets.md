# Cards, Debit Cards, Credit Cards, ATMs & Mobile Wallets

This document explains how Aurora Bank cards work and what customers should do when using cards, ATMs, contactless payments, and mobile wallets. For card fees and currency conversion, see [Fees, Interest & Foreign Exchange](06_fees_interest_and_exchange_rates.md). For card disputes, see [Disputes, Chargebacks & Fraud Claims](07_disputes_chargebacks_and_fraud_claims.md).

## 1. Card types

Aurora Bank may issue the following card types:

| Card type | Linked to | Main purpose | Key restrictions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Debit card | Current account | Spending the customer’s own available funds | No borrowing unless an overdraft is available. |
| Youth debit card | Youth current account | Controlled spending for customers aged 15 to 17 | Lower limits; no gambling merchants; guardian controls may apply. |
| Credit card | Separate credit-card account | Borrowing up to approved credit limit | Interest, minimum payments, and credit checks apply. |
| Virtual card | Current or credit-card account | Online and mobile wallet payments | May not support ATM cash withdrawal. |
| Replacement card | Existing card account | Replaces lost, stolen, damaged, or expired card | Old card may be cancelled when replacement is issued. |

A card remains property of Aurora Bank and must be returned or destroyed on request.

## 2. Receiving and activating a card

A physical card is normally sent by post to the customer’s verified address. The PIN may be issued separately or set in the mobile application. A customer must activate the card before first use by following the instructions provided.

Cards should be signed where a signature panel is present. Customers must keep the card, PIN, security code, mobile wallet credentials, and authentication device secure.

## 3. PIN and security code rules

Customers must never disclose their PIN, full card number plus security code, one-time passcodes, mobile wallet passcodes, or authentication credentials to anyone, including someone claiming to work for Aurora Bank.

Customers should not:

- write the PIN on the card or store it with the card;
- choose an obvious PIN such as date of birth or repeated digits;
- let another person use the card;
- approve a payment they do not recognize;
- enter card details after clicking a suspicious link;
- allow remote-access software to be installed at the request of an unknown caller.

Security responsibilities are described in [Security, Online Banking & Customer Responsibilities](09_security_online_banking_and_customer_responsibilities.md).

## 4. Contactless payments

Contactless payments allow low-value purchases without inserting the card or entering a PIN. Aurora Bank may occasionally require PIN entry or mobile-app confirmation to confirm that the customer still has possession of the card.

Contactless limits vary by country, merchant, and card type. Some merchants may apply lower limits. Offline contactless transactions, such as transport fares, may not appear immediately and can reduce the available balance later.

## 5. Card controls

Customers can use online banking or the mobile app to manage card controls, where available. Controls may include:

- temporary card freeze and unfreeze;
- online payments on or off;
- contactless payments on or off;
- ATM withdrawals on or off;
- gambling merchant restrictions;
- international card use restrictions;
- card spending notifications;
- replacement card request;
- PIN view or PIN change where supported.

A card freeze may stop many new transactions but may not stop all transactions. Offline transactions, delayed transit charges, merchant adjustments, subscriptions, and pre-authorized payments may still be processed.

## 6. Lost, stolen, damaged, or retained card

If a card is lost, stolen, retained by an ATM, or used without permission, the customer should immediately freeze the card in the app and contact Aurora Bank. Emergency contact options are listed in [Glossary, Contact Channels & Service Levels](12_glossary_contact_and_service_level.md).

Aurora Bank may cancel the card and issue a replacement. A replacement-card fee may apply unless the loss was caused by bank error, fraud, expiry, or another fee-waiver circumstance.

Customers should review recent transactions and report unauthorized card transactions promptly under [Disputes, Chargebacks & Fraud Claims](07_disputes_chargebacks_and_fraud_claims.md).

## 7. ATM cash withdrawals and deposits

Customers can use eligible cards at Aurora ATMs and participating network ATMs. ATM operators may charge their own fees or display conversion options. Aurora Bank does not control fees charged by non-Aurora ATM operators.

ATM services may include cash withdrawal, balance inquiry, PIN change, mini-statement, and cash deposit where available. Cash deposits at ATMs may be subject to counting, verification, counterfeit detection, and delayed availability.

Customers should check the ATM area for tampering, shield the PIN, take receipts, and contact Aurora Bank if the ATM does not dispense cash but the account is debited.

## 8. Card use abroad

Cards may be used abroad where the card scheme and merchant support the transaction. Customers should check travel controls before departure and ensure contact details are up to date.

Foreign currency transactions are converted into the account currency using the card scheme rate, Aurora Bank rate, or a merchant-selected rate, depending on how the transaction is processed. Fees and conversion rules are described in [Fees, Interest & Foreign Exchange](06_fees_interest_and_exchange_rates.md).

A merchant or ATM may offer dynamic currency conversion, allowing payment in the customer’s home currency. This rate is set by the merchant or ATM operator and may be less favorable than Aurora Bank’s standard conversion.

## 9. Pre-authorizations and pending transactions

Some merchants reserve an estimated amount before the final transaction is known. This is common for:

- hotels;
- car rental companies;
- fuel stations;
- restaurants with tips;
- online orders with split shipments;
- public transport and toll systems;
- security deposits.

A pre-authorization reduces the available balance but is not always the final amount. It normally expires automatically if the merchant does not settle it. Aurora Bank can remove a hold early only if the merchant provides release confirmation or scheme rules permit release.

## 10. Recurring card payments and subscriptions

A recurring card payment is an agreement between the customer and merchant to charge a card repeatedly. Examples include streaming services, gym memberships, software subscriptions, and app purchases.

To stop a recurring card payment, the customer should cancel with the merchant. Aurora Bank can help block future attempts from a merchant in some cases, but this may not end the customer’s contract. If the merchant keeps charging after cancellation, the customer may raise a dispute with evidence of cancellation.

## 11. Mobile wallets

Eligible Aurora cards can be added to approved mobile wallets. A mobile wallet uses a token instead of the physical card number. The customer must secure the device with passcode, biometrics, or another supported method.

If the device is lost or stolen, the customer should:

1. use the device provider’s lost-device tools where available;
2. remove the card from the wallet in online banking if possible;
3. contact Aurora Bank;
4. review recent transactions.

Removing a physical card does not always remove all wallet tokens automatically, and replacing a card may update tokens depending on the wallet provider.

## 12. Credit card specific rules

A credit card is a separate borrowing product. The customer must make at least the minimum payment by the due date. Interest may apply if the full statement balance is not paid by the due date or where the transaction type has no interest-free period.

Credit-card cash advances, money transfers, quasi-cash transactions, gambling transactions, and certain foreign-currency transactions may attract fees and immediate interest. Credit-card eligibility and affordability are described in [Loans, Overdrafts & Credit Assessment](05_loans_overdrafts_and_credit_assessment.md).

## 13. Card expiry and renewal

Cards are issued with an expiry date. Aurora Bank normally sends a renewal card before the current card expires, unless the account is closed, restricted, inactive, in serious arrears, or no longer eligible.

Customers should securely destroy expired cards by cutting through the chip and magnetic stripe. The old card may stop working at expiry even if a replacement has not arrived.

## 14. Merchant refunds

A merchant refund is initiated by the merchant and credited when received through the card scheme. A refund can take several business days and may not use the same exchange rate as the original transaction. If a refund is not received, the customer should first contact the merchant and then raise a card dispute if appropriate.
