# Privacy, Data Protection & Open Banking

This document explains how Aurora Bank collects, uses, shares, stores, and protects customer personal data. It is written in a GDPR-style format for a fictional EEA bank. Security practices are described in [Security, Online Banking & Customer Responsibilities](09_security_online_banking_and_customer_responsibilities.md).

## 1. Who controls customer data

Aurora Bank plc is the controller for most personal data processed in connection with personal banking products. Some services may involve joint controllers or independent controllers, such as card schemes, credit reference agencies, fraud prevention agencies, payment systems, insurers, and open banking providers.

A customer can contact the Data Protection Office through the channels listed in [Glossary, Contact Channels & Service Levels](12_glossary_contact_and_service_level.md).

## 2. Data we collect

Aurora Bank may collect the following categories of data:

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Identity data | Name, date of birth, nationality, identity document number, photograph, signature. |
| Contact data | Address, phone number, email address, communication preferences. |
| Financial data | Account balances, transactions, income, expenses, debts, tax residency, source of funds. |
| Product data | Account type, card type, loan applications, overdraft limits, statement history. |
| Device and security data | IP address, device identifiers, login times, authentication method, fraud signals. |
| Communications data | Calls, secure messages, branch notes, complaint records, chatbot transcripts. |
| Special category data | Health, disability, vulnerability, or accessibility information only where necessary and lawful. |
| Public and third-party data | Credit bureau records, sanctions lists, company registers, fraud databases, public records. |

We may collect data directly from customers, from transactions, from devices, from public sources, from employers or merchants when relevant, from payment schemes, from credit reference agencies, and from fraud prevention partners.

## 3. Why we use data

Aurora Bank uses customer data to:

- verify identity and address;
- open and operate accounts;
- process payments and card transactions;
- assess credit applications and affordability;
- prevent fraud, scams, money laundering, terrorist financing, and sanctions breaches;
- comply with tax, accounting, legal, regulatory, and reporting obligations;
- provide customer support and resolve complaints;
- manage risk and improve products;
- send service messages and legal notices;
- personalize digital banking features;
- provide marketing where permitted;
- support vulnerable customers where the customer asks for help or law permits processing.

## 4. Legal bases for processing

We rely on different legal bases depending on the activity:

| Legal basis | Example |
|---|---|
| Contract | Opening an account, processing payments, issuing cards, providing statements. |
| Legal obligation | Identity checks, tax reporting, anti-money laundering monitoring, regulatory reporting. |
| Legitimate interests | Fraud prevention, service improvement, network security, internal analytics. |
| Consent | Certain marketing preferences, optional open banking permissions, optional biometric settings where required. |
| Vital interests | Emergency situations where life or safety is at risk. |
| Public interest or substantial public interest | Financial crime prevention and support for vulnerable customers where applicable. |

Consent can normally be withdrawn, but withdrawal does not affect processing already carried out or processing needed for contract or legal obligations.

## 5. Credit checks and automated decisions

When a customer requests credit, Aurora Bank may obtain information from credit reference agencies and fraud prevention databases. We may use automated decisioning to assess eligibility, affordability, risk, fraud indicators, and product pricing.

A customer can request human review of certain automated decisions where legally required. Aurora Bank may still decline credit after human review if product criteria, affordability, fraud, or legal requirements are not met.

Credit assessment is explained in [Loans, Overdrafts & Credit Assessment](05_loans_overdrafts_and_credit_assessment.md).

## 6. Sharing data

Aurora Bank may share data with:

- payment systems and card schemes;
- beneficiary and sending banks;
- credit reference agencies;
- fraud prevention agencies;
- regulators, tax authorities, courts, law enforcement, and ombudsman bodies;
- service providers such as IT hosting, card production, statement printing, call-center systems, and document verification providers;
- debt collection agencies and legal advisers where permitted;
- insurers or benefit providers for packaged accounts;
- open banking providers where the customer authorizes access;
- persons appointed by the customer, such as attorneys, guardians, executors, or authorized representatives.

We do not sell customer transaction data to advertisers.

## 7. International transfers

Some service providers, payment systems, card schemes, or correspondent banks may process data outside the customer’s country or the EEA. Aurora Bank uses appropriate safeguards where required, such as adequacy decisions, standard contractual clauses, transfer risk assessments, or legal derogations for international payments.

International payments may require sending customer and beneficiary information through correspondent banking networks.

## 8. Data retention

Aurora Bank retains data only as long as necessary for the purpose collected and legal obligations. Indicative retention periods:

| Record type | Typical retention period |
|---|---:|
| Account opening and identity records | 5-10 years after relationship ends, depending on law. |
| Transaction records | 7-10 years after transaction or account closure. |
| Credit applications | Up to 6 years after decision, or longer for active agreements. |
| Complaint records | 5-7 years after closure. |
| Call recordings | 6 months to 7 years depending on call type and legal need. |
| Marketing preferences | Until changed, plus suppression records to honor opt-out. |
| Vulnerability support notes | As long as needed for support and reviewed regularly. |

We may retain data longer where required for litigation, regulatory investigation, fraud prevention, sanctions compliance, or legal hold.

## 9. Customer rights

Subject to legal limits, customers may have the right to:

- access their personal data;
- correct inaccurate data;
- erase data where retention is no longer required;
- restrict processing;
- object to processing based on legitimate interests;
- receive certain data in portable format;
- withdraw consent;
- object to direct marketing;
- request human review of certain automated decisions;
- complain to a data protection authority.

Some rights may be limited where data is needed for account operation, legal obligations, financial crime prevention, legal claims, or rights of others.

## 10. Open banking

Open banking allows a customer to give a regulated third-party provider access to account information or payment initiation services. Aurora Bank will provide access only after the customer gives permission through the required secure process.

Important open banking points:

- Aurora Bank will never ask the customer to share online banking passwords with a third-party provider.
- The customer can withdraw open banking consent through Aurora online banking or the third-party provider.
- Revoking access does not delete data already received by the third-party provider.
- The third-party provider is responsible for its own use of data after it receives it.
- Payment initiation through open banking may still require strong customer authentication.

Customers should use only regulated providers and should check the provider name carefully before approving access.

## 11. Marketing preferences

Service messages are not marketing and may be sent even if a customer opts out of marketing. Service messages include security alerts, account notices, regulatory notices, fraud warnings, statement availability, and product changes.

Customers can choose whether to receive marketing by email, SMS, phone, post, or in-app messages. Opting out of marketing does not stop personalized product features inside online banking where they are part of service delivery.

## 12. Vulnerable customer data

Aurora Bank may record support needs where a customer asks for help or where it is necessary to provide fair service. Examples include preferred communication method, accessibility format, trusted contact, temporary financial difficulty, bereavement, illness, or digital-support need.

We will use this information to provide support and avoid foreseeable harm. We will review support notes periodically and delete them when no longer needed unless retention is required by law.

## 13. Cookies and digital analytics

Aurora Bank websites and apps may use cookies, device storage, and analytics tools for security, authentication, fraud prevention, service functionality, performance monitoring, and optional personalization. Customers can manage non-essential cookies where required by law.

Security cookies and fraud-prevention signals may be required for online banking and cannot always be disabled.

## 14. Data accuracy and customer responsibilities

Customers should keep personal data accurate and current. Incorrect address, phone number, email, tax residency, or identity information may delay payments, card delivery, account recovery, loan assessment, and legal notices.

Changes to customer information are described in [Account Opening, Eligibility & Customer Verification](01_account_opening_and_kyc.md).
